# Marketing claims

The FTC (and EU consumer law) treats a claim as a promise: you need evidence **before** you make it.

| Claim type | Risk | Rewrite to |
|---|---|---|
| "GDPR compliant", "HIPAA compliant", "SOC 2 certified" | High: a legal status, easy to disprove | Describe what you do: "We sign a DPA with every customer and host data in the EU." Say "SOC 2" only with a current report. |
| "100% secure", "bank-level security", "unhackable" | High: nothing is 100% secure | Name the controls: "Encrypted in transit and at rest. Two-factor sign-in." |
| "#1", "the best", "world's first" | Medium: needs a fair comparison you can show | "Rated 4.8/5 on G2 (March 2026)" with the source, or drop the superlative. |
| "Trusted by 10,000+ teams", "3x faster" | Medium: needs current numbers and a method | Use the real figure and date, or remove it. |
| "Free forever", "guaranteed" | Medium: a promise you must keep | Say what's free today and what the guarantee covers. |
| Testimonials and reviews | Must be real, from real customers, and say if they were paid or given something | Label paid ones; never write or AI-generate reviews. |

When the user keeps a claim, ask what the evidence is and tell them to keep it on file.
